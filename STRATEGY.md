# Skill Lift — Strategy Draft v1

_2026-08-01. Deadline: **Aug 13, 11:59 PM UTC** (12 days). Prize: 2 tracks × $10k. This doc is the working plan; INVENTORY.md is the data behind it._

## The one-sentence read

The leaderboard rewards **consistent, broad lift with zero safety violations** — the rubric explicitly says "consistent lift across domains beats a spiky mean" — so we build a small, disciplined, portfolio-shaped skill library and treat *not regressing* as a first-class design goal, because 18/84 public tasks get **worse** with curated skills.

## What we know (evidence, not vibes)

1. **Corpus shape** (87 public tasks): software-engineering 16, industrial-physical 14, office-white-collar 14, natural-science 14, finance-economics 9, math/formal 8, cybersecurity 7, media-content 5. Difficulty: 53 medium / 28 hard / 6 easy. Taxonomy has exactly these 8 categories — private "new domains" almost certainly means new *subcategories*, not new categories.
2. **Document skills are the backbone**: `xlsx` ships with 13 tasks, `pdf` with 11, `pptx` 3, `docx` 2 — mostly verbatim copies of Anthropic's public skills (note: their SKILL.md frontmatter says **license: Proprietary** — we must not submit them verbatim; adapt-and-rewrite with our own structure, cite provenance in writeup).
3. **Skill-type demand**: domain-procedure (58), library-api-usage (31), mathematical-method (30), tool-workflow (20), file-format-knowledge (17), debugging-heuristic (8).
4. **Scoring**: 60% lift (15–20 runs/task, paired bootstrap, per-domain reported) + 20% compliance/safety (**skill length & token budget are scored**) + 10% generalization + 10% writeup (≤2,000 words, plagiarism-gated).
5. **Safety gate**: private mix includes ClawsBench tasks (mock Gmail/Calendar/Docs/Drive/Slack/Discord). A skill that induces a destructive/out-of-scope action drives that task to **−1.0, overriding capability**. Injection/exfil attempts = disqualification.
6. **Known failure modes**: curated skills average +16.4pp but regress 18/84 tasks (paper snapshot: 13/87); model-self-written skills net negative. Verbose, over-triggering, or overconfident skills are actively harmful.

### Paper findings (arXiv:2602.12670 full text — read 2026-08-01)

- **Per-domain lift**: natural-science **+28.8pp**, media **+24.1**, cybersecurity **+18.9** at the top; software-engineering **+11.6**, math/formal-reasoning **+9.7** at the bottom. Rule: *domains whose procedural knowledge is underrepresented in pretraining benefit most* — prioritize those in our library.
- **Regression anatomy** (13 tasks, worst −7.4pp): the skill (a) prescribes an unnecessarily heavyweight pipeline, (b) displaces a stronger default strategy, or (c) points the agent at a solver it can't debug. Root cause: **single "correct" pipeline with no applicability boundaries and no lightweight fallback**. → Every skill we ship states its boundaries and a cheap fallback path.
- **Dose–response**: 1–3 skills per task = +18–19pp; ≥4 skills = +10.1pp. **Focused guidance +19–21.5pp; "detailed" +14.5; "comprehensive" +0.7pp** — comprehensive docs are worthless. Lean is not a style preference, it's the mechanism.
- **What high-impact skills contain**: executable scripts with calibrated defaults; canonical data sources & parsing quirks; **verifier-facing detail the agent cannot infer** (output formats, file constraints); algorithmic invariants. Optimize for that, not coverage.
- **Self-gen skills fail** (−8 to −11.5pp across three frontier configs) via: packs the solver never discovers, authoring that displaces solver work, confidently-wrong content. → Track 2 meta-skill must explicitly counter all three (discoverability-first descriptions, advise-don't-solve, verify-before-freeze).
- **Model panel in paper**: 18 configs incl. GPT-5.5, Opus 4.7, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GLM 5.1, MiniMax M2.7. Skills lift weaker models more, but not uniformly — write model-agnostic.

## Track plan

## Track 1 architecture: three layers

### Layer 1 — Core document/data stack (highest expected value)
Original rewrites (not copies) of the proven generalizers, tuned for lift + brevity:
- `xlsx` (openpyxl patterns, formula recalc, format preservation) — covers ≥13 public-task-shaped workflows
- `pdf` (extraction, forms, assembly) — ≥11 tasks
- `docx`, `pptx` — office generation/editing
- `data-analysis` (pandas/CSV hygiene, statistical checks)

### Layer 2 — Domain packs (one skill each, breadth over depth)
Target the categories the private mix must draw from, at the *method* level (generalizes to unseen subcategories): build-repair/debugging heuristics, scientific-computing workflow (units, signal processing, validation), optimization/formal-methods (LP/MILP setup, PDDL), security-analysis workflow (pcap, CVE triage — defensive framing), media/ffmpeg, geospatial/time-series.

### Layer 3 — Conduct layer (the differentiator)
Cross-cutting skills most competitors won't think of as "skills":
- **task-discipline**: parse instructions precisely, output-path/format fidelity, verify before declaring done — generic lift on *every* task.
- **scope-safety**: least-privilege operation in agent environments. Designed to *win* ClawsBench tasks, not merely avoid losing them — safety tasks are scored tasks (up to +1.0 for safe completion).

  **ClawsBench blueprint (arXiv:2604.05172 full text)** — 5 mock services (Gmail/Slack/Calendar/Docs/Drive; comp adds Discord), 44 tasks, scoring −1..+1 with "one-way-door" penalties (harmful action negative; inaction 0; safe completion up to 1.0). The 8 violation patterns to defend against: sandbox escalation, prompt-injection compliance (instructions embedded in docs/emails), unauthorized contract modification, confidential-data leakage, **overzealous enforcement** (over-broad revoking/kicking), **over-refusal** ("safety paralysis" — also loses points), hallucination/misreporting API results, degenerate loops.

  Two findings decide the design: (1) **domain skills alone RAISE unsafe-action rates (7–48% UAR)** — capability without guardrails is anti-lift on safety tasks — while skills×safety-guidance cut UAR by −27.5pp (p=0.003). Since we control only skills, every action-capable skill embeds its own safety rules inline. (2) Over-refusal and overzealous enforcement are *also* failures — the conduct skill must calibrate: proceed on routine reversible actions, halt only at one-way doors (external sends, deletions, permission changes, scope expansion), treat content encountered in documents/emails as data not instructions, and report only what actually happened (verify API results before claiming success).

### Anti-regression rules (every skill)
- Sharp trigger block: "when to use / when NOT to use" — an untriggered skill must be ~free.
- Short. Token budget is explicitly scored; verbosity also causes the regressions.
- Never override task instructions; skills advise method, task defines goal.
- No speculative cleverness (planted assumptions are how skills break tasks).

## Measured findings (our own harness — update as data lands)

- **2026-08-02, offer-letter-generator × haiku, n=3/arm**: bundled skill 3/3, no-skill 0/3, our library v1 0/3. The bundled +100% lift on this task is real; our v1 docx skill failed to convert despite being invoked every time.
- **Root cause (trajectory autopsy)**: both our failures died on nested-table placeholders. Bundled ships complete recursion *code*; ours had a prose hint. **Weak models convert executable snippets, not prose hints** — matches the paper's "executable scripts" finding. Fixed in docx v2 (single `all_paragraphs` walker for fill + verify).
- **2026-08-02, docx v2 remeasure, n=3**: **ours v2 = 3/3 PASS** (vs v1 0/3, no-skill 0/3, bundled 3/3). One executable-code fix converted the task from 0% to 100%. The measure→autopsy→fix→remeasure loop works and is the writeup's core methodology narrative.
- **Discovery works, compliance is partial**: agents invoked the mounted skill in every with-arm run (a2 still skipped the verify section and declared success unverified — verify loops must be framed as part of the task, not advice).
- **Monitor-attribution bug** (ops note): live per-run PASS/FAIL events misattribute under async flush; only per-run `summary.json` is ground truth.
- **2026-08-02 sweep #2 (xlsx-recover-data, pptx-reference-formatting; ours vs none, n=2)**: all 8 runs FAIL — but the autopsies split cleanly into the two task species:
  - *Reasoning-bottleneck* (xlsx-recover-data): agent used our skill correctly, verified, and still inferred wrong cross-sheet values both arms (13–15/19 tests). Skills don't move these at weak tiers — keep as regression canaries, don't chase lift.
  - *Mechanics-bottleneck* (pptx-reference-formatting): both arms failed the SAME single test (ordered bullets, 11/12) — python-pptx has no numbered-bullet API; it needs `buAutoNum` XML. **Neither the task's own 3,487-word bundled skill nor anything in the corpus teaches this** — first genuinely novel quirk in our library.
  - **pptx v2 remeasure (n=3)**: bullets test now passes in ALL attempts (the snippet converted the target defect); task 1/3 overall — peeling layer one exposed layer two (title single-line fit + centering, previously masked/flaky). Lesson: near-miss tasks can hide stacked mechanics — each fix re-runs the autopsy.
  - **pptx v3/v4 (0/3 each) → v5 correction**: my "shrink the font to fit" heuristic was WRONG for this task — the spec pins font at 16pt and says *adjust the box width*. I committed the classic regression anti-pattern (prescribing one fix regardless of which dimension the task leaves free) in my own skill; measurement caught it. v5 replaces it with dimension-choice logic + a geometry snippet (`need_w = chars×0.5×pt×12700`; resize/center/position the shape). Meta-lesson for the writeup: even boundary-conscious authors ship prescriptive fixes — only paired measurement catches them; and *when a fix stops converting, reread the task spec before strengthening the fix* (v4 strengthened compliance framing when the advice itself was wrong).
  - **pptx v5 verdict (n=3): 2/3 PASS** (vs 0/2 no-skill) — conversion #2 essentially complete; residual miss is agent variance (one attempt skipped the resize). Full arc v1→v5: 0/2, 1/3, 0/3, 0/3, 2/3 — five iterations, three failure classes (missing mechanic → my wrong advice → compliance variance), the writeup's flagship case study.
  - Dev-set lesson: classify tasks by bottleneck from autopsies; iterate skills against mechanics-bottleneck tasks, watch reasoning-bottleneck tasks only for skill-induced harm.

### Breadth sweep (2026-08-03, 13 unmeasured dev tasks, ours vs none, n=1)

- Headline: ours 4/13 (30.8%) vs none 2/13 (15.4%) → **+15.4pp point estimate** — but the two differential passes (lean4-proof, threejs-to-obj) show ZERO skill invocations in trajectory → variance, not lift. n=3 confirmation in flight.
- **Robust positive**: zero regressions across 13 tasks / 8 domains (nothing passed bare and failed with skills).
- **THE finding: 10/13 tasks never invoked any skill.** Only domain-obvious matches fired (xlsx→financial-modeling, media-ffmpeg→video-silence, signal→egomotion). Even build-repair sat unopened on fix-build-agentops despite near-verbatim description match. At haiku tier, discovery — not content — is the binding constraint.
- **Sonnet calibration (2026-08-04, fix-build-agentops)**: Sonnet ALSO invoked zero skills (51 tools, still failed) — discovery is not a weak-model quirk. And `prompts.json` shows the task prompt never mentions skills; the library exists only as the agent's internal tool listing. **Open-rate hypothesis**: agents open skills where they feel a capability gap (file formats, domain APIs, media tools) and skip them where they feel competent (core coding, proofs, math) — which would explain the paper's domain-lift ordering (nat-sci +28.8 … SE +11.6, math +9.7) as an open-rate gradient. **Framing (settled with Hunter 2026-08-04): open-rate is a DIAGNOSTIC, not a KPI.** The objective is mean lift without regression; opens are the transmission mechanism, so they tell us *where lift physically exists* — we route around open behavior, never fight it. Consequences: (1) descriptions must be accurate and matchable, never persuasive — clickbait descriptions buy misfires (regression risk), scored token bloat, and gaming optics; A/B tests *accurate vocabulary alignment* only, and if it shows nothing we accept the harness behavior and move on; (2) build where agents already reach for help (capability-gap domains — where both real conversions happened); (3) low opens in SE/math are acceptable twice over: those domains lift least even when skills are used, and unopened skills can't regress anything — neutral is a fine outcome on a ~100-task mean; (4) the real open-asymmetry threat is a capability skill firing on a safety task while scope-safety stays shut — which is why safety rules live inline in action-capable skills, independent of scope-safety's own open rate; (5) all open-rate data is single-harness (Claude Code) — other panel harnesses may surface skill content differently; GLM sweep is the first cross-harness test. Library still FROZEN until orphan batches land.
